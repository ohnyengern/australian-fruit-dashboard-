Australian Fruit Production

01

A Snapshot of Eight Selected Fruits

Oranges dominated the harvest among the eight selected fruits, reaching 572,540 tonnes in 2024–25. The combined harvest of these eight fruits reached 1.94 million tonnes, with oranges alone accounting for almost 30% of the total. By comparison, strawberries and blueberries together accounted for only about 5.5% of the harvest. This substantial difference in production volume highlights the uneven distribution of production across the eight selected fruit categories in Australia.

02

How Fruit Production Has Changed Over Time

Orange and mandarin production followed the clearest upward trajectories from 2020–21 to 2024–25. Orange production increased every year, rising from 473,697 tonnes to 572,540 tonnes.

Banana production moved in the opposite direction, declining from 402,982 tonnes to 368,701 tonnes. This contrast suggests that overall fruit production growth was driven by specific categories rather than by a uniform industry-wide increase.

2021–22 represents the percentage change from 2020–21 to 2021–22.

Annual production growth varied considerably across the eight fruits, with some growing consistently while others fluctuated sharply. Mandarins and oranges recorded positive growth every year, while avocados, blueberries and strawberries experienced alternating periods of growth and decline.

Notably, several fruits recorded strong growth in 2023–24, but table grapes declined by 19.9% before rebounding by 22.6% in 2024–25. This recovery did not fully reverse the earlier loss: the 2024–25 harvest remained 1.8% below its 2022–23 level because the rebound started from a smaller production base.

Production rankings remained largely unchanged from 2020–21 to 2024–25, despite differences in growth across the eight fruits. Oranges, bananas and apples consistently held the top three positions, maintaining their dominance in production volume throughout the five-year period.

The only ranking change occurred in 2023–24, when mandarins overtook table grapes to claim fourth place. Mandarins retained this position in 2024–25, reflecting their stronger production growth relative to table grapes over the five-year period.

Each fruit’s 2020–21 production is set as the 0% baseline.

Avocados recorded the fastest relative growth of 68.3%, reflecting substantial expansion from a relatively small production base. Although oranges added nearly 99,000 tonnes to Australia's harvest, their percentage growth was only 20.9%. This shows that larger crops can contribute more tonnes without growing as quickly in relative terms.

Mandarins grew by 44.8%, overtaking table grapes in production volume by 2024–25. Meanwhile, bananas declined by 8.5% but remained the second-largest crop among the eight selected fruits. This highlights how faster-growing fruits can move up the rankings, while high-volume crops can retain their positions despite declining production.

Fruit production is not evenly distributed across Australia, and the degree of concentration differs considerably between crops. Some fruits rely heavily on a single producing state, while others have a broader production base across multiple states.

These differences partly reflect Australia's diverse climate and growing conditions. Tropical regions favour warm-climate fruits, while cooler southern regions support temperate crops. As a result, the geographical distribution of fruit production varies according to the growing requirements of each crop.

Fruit production growth varied significantly across Australian states between 2020–21 and 2024–25. Avocados showed the strongest regional contrast, with production more than doubling in Western Australia and South Australia while declining by over 50% in Victoria. This reveals how avocado production can expand rapidly in some states while declining sharply in others.

Not all fruits followed such contrasting regional trends. Banana production declined across its main producing states, while apple production increased modestly across its major growing regions. This suggests that some fruits experienced broadly similar changes across states, while others showed much greater regional variation.

04

The Value Behind Fruit Production

Higher production volume does not necessarily result in greater production value. While high-volume crops such as oranges lead in production tonnage, they generate relatively low value per tonne. In contrast, table grapes, avocados and blueberries achieve substantial production value from smaller harvests. This highlights how the economic contribution of fruit production depends not only on the quantity harvested, but also on the market value of different fruits.

05

From Australian Farms to the World

The domestic fresh market absorbs nearly 62% of production across the eight selected fruits, but their market destinations vary considerably. Bananas are supplied almost entirely domestically, while table grapes are more export-oriented, with over 60% of their production exported fresh. Fresh exports account for around 22% of the combined harvest, driven mainly by oranges, table grapes and mandarins. The 16% goes to processing, with oranges and apples contributing most of this volume.

Australia's fresh-fruit exports are strongly concentrated in Asian markets, but the mix of export destinations differs between fruits. China is a major destination for shipments of table grapes, mandarins and oranges, while Southeast Asian markets such as Singapore, Malaysia and Thailand receive a wider variety of Australian fruits. Farther west, avocados also reach the United Arab Emirates, extending Australia's fresh-fruit trade connections into the Middle East.

Bananas are excluded because no fresh exports were recorded.

Average export value per tonne increased for six fruits between 2020–21 and 2024–25. Strawberries recorded the largest increase of 93.1%, despite a 25.3% decline in export volume. This may reflect stronger international demand for Australian strawberries, potentially allowing them to command higher prices in overseas markets.

In contrast, avocados were the only fruit to record a decline in average export value per tonne, falling by 12.6%. Despite a substantial increase in avocado export volume, its average export value per tonne decreased. This may be due to increased avocado supply in overseas markets, where greater competition could have led to lower export prices.
