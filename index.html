Australian Fruit Production

01

A Snapshot of Eight Selected Fruits

Oranges dominated the harvest among the eight selected fruits, reaching 572,540 tonnes in the year ending June 2025. They supplied almost 30% of the combined harvest of 1.94 million tonnes.

By comparison, strawberries and blueberries together accounted for only about 5.5%. The large difference in volume shows that production is unevenly distributed across the selected fruit categories in Australia.

02

How Fruit Production Has Changed Over Time

Orange and mandarin production followed the clearest upward trajectories from 2021 to 2025. Orange production increased every year, rising from 473,697 tonnes to 572,540 tonnes.

Bananas moved in the opposite direction, declining from 402,982 tonnes to 368,701 tonnes. This contrast suggests that overall fruit production growth is being driven by specific categories rather than by a uniform industry-wide increase.

Annual production growth varied considerably across the eight fruits, with some growing consistently while others fluctuated sharply. Mandarins and oranges recorded positive growth every year, while avocados, blueberries and strawberries experienced alternating periods of growth and decline.

Notably, several fruits recorded strong growth in 2024, but table grapes declined by 19.9% before rebounding by 22.6% in 2025. This recovery did not fully reverse the earlier loss: the 2025 harvest remained 1.8% below its 2023 level because the rebound started from a smaller production base.

Production rankings remained largely unchanged between 2021 and 2025, despite differences in growth across the eight fruits. Oranges, bananas and apples consistently held the top three positions throughout this five-year period.

These established high-volume crops maintained their dominance. The only change in rankings occurred in 2024, when mandarins overtook table grapes and retained fourth place in 2025. Both fruits finished above their 2021 production levels.

Australia's fruit production expanded unevenly between 2021 and 2025, changing the relative importance of different crops. Avocados recorded the fastest percentage growth, while oranges contributed the largest increase in production volume. Mandarins also gained ground, overtaking table grapes despite starting 32,000 tonnes behind in 2021. Meanwhile, bananas remained a major crop despite declining production, showing that high production volume does not necessarily translate into continued growth.

Fruit production is not evenly distributed across Australia, and the degree of concentration differs considerably between crops. Some fruits rely heavily on a single producing state, while others have a broader production base across multiple states.

These differences partly reflect Australia's diverse climate and growing conditions. Tropical regions favour warm-climate fruits, while cooler southern regions support temperate crops. As a result, the geographical distribution of fruit production varies according to the growing requirements of each crop.

Fruit production growth varied across Australian states between the years ending June 2021 and June 2025. Avocado production more than doubled in Western Australia and South Australia but declined by over 50% in Victoria. In Tasmania, avocado production rose from zero to 17 tonnes, but its percentage change cannot be calculated because production started at zero.

Banana production declined across its main producing states, whereas apple production increased modestly across its major growing regions. These differences show that growth patterns vary between both states and fruits, with some experiencing widespread increases while others face broader declines.

04

The Value Behind Fruit Production

Higher production volume does not necessarily result in greater production value. While high-volume crops such as oranges lead in production tonnage, they generate relatively low value per tonne. In contrast, table grapes, avocados and blueberries achieve substantial production value from smaller harvests. This highlights how the economic contribution of fruit production depends not only on the quantity harvested, but also on the market value of different fruits.

05

From Australian Farms to the World

The domestic fresh market absorbs nearly 62% of production across the eight selected fruits, but their market destinations vary considerably. Bananas are supplied almost entirely domestically, while table grapes are more export-oriented, with over 60% of their production exported fresh. Fresh exports account for around 22% of the combined harvest, driven mainly by oranges, table grapes and mandarins. The 16% goes to processing, with oranges and apples contributing most of this volume.

Australia's fresh-fruit exports are strongly concentrated in Asian markets, but different fruits have distinct trading patterns. China is a major destination for table grapes, mandarins and oranges, while Southeast Asian markets such as Singapore, Malaysia and Thailand receive a wider variety of Australian fruits. Beyond Asia, avocados also reach the United Arab Emirates, demonstrating Australia's trade connections with the Middle East.

Bananas are excluded because no fresh exports were recorded.

Average export value per tonne increased for six fruits between 2021 and 2025. Strawberries recorded the largest increase of 93.1%, despite a 25.3% decline in export volume. This may reflect stronger international demand for Australian strawberries, potentially allowing them to command higher prices in overseas markets.

In contrast, avocados were the only fruit to record a decline in average export value per tonne, falling by 12.6%. Despite a substantial increase in avocado export volume, its average export value per tonne decreased. This may be due to increased avocado supply in overseas markets, where greater competition could have led to lower export prices.
